# BOLT: Consolidated Limitations Table

> **Canonical location for limitations disclosures.** The table below is the
> single authoritative list. `docs/evaluation.md` §8 (Residual Limitations) and
> §4 carry prose cross-references to this file; `docs/bolt/theorem.md`,
> `docs/bolt/threat_model.md`, and `docs/bolt/detection_eval.md` carry
> per-item disclosures at the point of relevance. Nothing here contradicts those
> in-context treatments; this table consolidates them.

| # | Limitation | Consequence | Mitigation / disclosure location |
|---|-----------|-------------|----------------------------------|
| L1 | **Reinstate-digest replay nuance.** `executeReinstate` reuses the same `docHash` field as `executeRegister`; no `OpType` field is folded into the signed digest. A valid `k`-of-`n` register signature over `(validator, docHash, ttl)` is structurally replayable as a reinstate proof for the same `(validator, docHash)` pair (different op, same digest). | An attacker holding `m`-of-`n` governance approvals for a register op could present the same countersignatures as reinstate evidence, reviving a previously revoked key without a new governance round, provided the same `docHash` is reused. | Mitigated by: (a) the `m`-of-`n` governance quorum (`m = 3`) required for both ops, which the attacker must still satisfy; (b) the **fresh-`docHash` convention**: production deployments should always pass a new `docHash` for each reinstate (encoding the key-rotation or review timestamp), making the register digest stale; (c) an `OpType` salt in the digest is the correct engineering fix and is flagged as a production hardening item. See `docs/bolt/threat_model.md` (Governance scope); `test/contracts/registryv2.spec.ts:215` (fresh opId per round). |
| L2 | **Benign-value denial 50.48% under LOCKED.** The full real benign CCTX stream (82,685 transfers, $4.35B, Jan–Dec 2022) replayed through the locked limiter+breaker denies or delays 50.48% of benign value: almost entirely the 92 whale transfers larger than bucket capacity `B` (48.43% of all benign value; largest benign transfer $396M ≈ 127×B). | The extraction bound is a **policy choice about the whale tail**, not a free lunch. Operators sizing `B`, `r`, `T` to the Ronin-calibrated locked defaults must accept structural denial of large legitimate transfers. | Quantified and reported in `docs/bolt/benign_utility.md` and `docs/evaluation.md` §7b. The 42-config Pareto sweep, **re-derived against the proved envelope** (J15, `docs/logs/j15_envelope_rederivation.log`), shows that no swept configuration buys a *materially* sub-Ronin envelope at single-digit benign denial. The literal earlier claim, that *none* achieves both, was an artefact of the superseded `T + r·Δ` ordinate and is **withdrawn**: `B = 150,000, r = 1` reaches an envelope of $477.9M (88.5% of the Ronin loss) at 8.68% denied-or-delayed, of which none is denial and all is delay, at a 99th-percentile wait of 23,787 s. That is a property of the whale-concentrated stream, not of the mechanism. Latency cost is negligible (P99 delay = 0 s; max 35 min). `docs/logs/scenario_8_benign_utility.log`. |
| L3 | **≈ 7.0 benign false halts/month at ARL₀ = 371,868 s.** The sequential detector's measured `ARL₀` on the real held-out benign CCTX cadence implies ≈ 7.0 monitor-trip halts per 30-day month under locked params (the only reachable auto-halt path at LOCKED). This figure **doubled** from ≈ 3.5/month when the Phase-22 B2 de-circularized selection replaced `cap=1.5` with `cap=2.0` (φ 0.0013 → 0.0025). | Every spurious halt suspends all bridge withdrawals until `m`-of-`n` governance resets. At 7 halts/month, operator governance must respond within hours to maintain acceptable availability. Honest delta: the improvement over the budget-matched value z-score (≈ 114 halts/month at the same train budget) is the reason near-zero φ makes latching tolerable at all. | Disclosed in `docs/bolt/threat_model.md` §B (Benign false-halt frequency) and `docs/evaluation.md` §7c. The de-circularized selection is the methodologically correct choice even at this availability cost; the doubled rate is propagated through every figure that depends on it. `docs/logs/scenario_6_detection_eval.log:71`. |
| L4 | **Labels-by-construction (construct-validity threat).** The incident-positive labels for the CCTX evaluation dataset are XChainWatcher's own unmatched-withdrawal output. The xchainwatcher-rules comparator's recall ≈ 1.0 on our TEST split is therefore labels-by-construction, not a measured detection capability. Mislabeling runs both ways. | No independent adjudication of ground truth exists. The comparator comparison is a capability/deployment-cost contrast, not a fair classifier shoot-out. Per-row superiority claims are not asserted in either direction. | Flagged at every point of use: `docs/bolt/related_work.md` §1, `docs/bolt/detection_eval.md` §Threats to validity, `docs/bolt/h1_scorecard.md` row 3 caveats. |
| L5 | **Single-incident-class scope.** The evaluation covers two real bridge incidents (Ronin, Nomad): row-level statistics are powered, but incident-level generalisation is `n = 2`. The H1 canonical low-and-slow drain is synthetic-injected (ILLUSTRATIVE-labelled). | Recall and AUC on the Nomad structuring cohort (per-tx 0.633) represents a single incident class (repeated mid-six-figure transfers). Dust-level low-and-slow drains from other incident classes are not empirically tested. The pre-fix "dust sub-cohort" was a mixed-decimals artifact and is empty under corrected units. | Disclosed in `docs/bolt/detection_eval.md` §5, `docs/evaluation.md` §8 item 7. Real-Nomad evidence is explicitly labelled mixed-structuring, not dust. |
| L6 | **LOCKED value-trip unreachable by construction (layered defence).** Under the canonical LOCKED ledger (`B = 1000, r = 1, T = 5000, W = 3600`), the on-chain value trip is **structurally unreachable**: `B + r·W = 4,600 < T = 5,000`, so `windowOutflow()` can never exceed `T`. All 40 LOCKED on-chain scenario-5 rows record `trip=no`. The monitor signal trip is the only live auto-halt path at LOCKED. | The synchronous `checkAndRecord` value trip, the defense-in-depth headroom, is never exercised under LOCKED. The fast-drain envelope `X ≤ B + r·(W + Δ) = 4,660 ETH` holds and is enforced by the limiter alone; indeed `B + r·W = 4,600 < T` is exactly *why* the trip is dormant. (The registered `X_max ≤ T + r·Δ` is superseded; [[theorem]] §14.1.) The live proof path runs through the monitor signal, and with the monitor down **and** the trip unreachable nothing halts the attacker within the epoch; see [[theorem]] Objection 1. Non-vacuity is supplied by the separately labelled ALT_TRIP config (`T = 4,000`, giving 5 genuine `trip=YES` rows). | Disclosed and foregrounded in `docs/bolt/theorem.md` §14.1.2 (LOCKED-parameter reachability), `docs/bolt/h1_scorecard.md` row 4 caveats, `docs/evaluation.md` §Headline Results (bound non-vacuity row). The choice is deliberate: LOCKED trades value-trip reachability for zero benign value-trip halts (scenario_8: 0 halts over the full benign stream vs 6 under ALT_TRIP). |
| L7 | **Patient bound is an expectation; sub-drift extraction unbounded.** `D = ARL₁` is a *mean* detection delay; `E[X_slow] ≤ B + r·D + r·Δ` bounds **mean** patient extraction, not every run. Individual detection delays have a right tail; per-run extraction inherits that tail. An attacker whose drift stays **below** the detector's detectability threshold is outside the bound entirely: extraction is then floored only by the limiter envelope `B + r·t`, linear in time. | No deterministic per-run cap exists for the patient regime (the quantile companion `B + r·D_q + r·Δ` is stated but `D_q` is unmeasured). Sub-detectability drains are bounded only by rate `r` until external governance intervention. At `r = 1 ETH/s`, Ronin scale sub-drift would take ≳ 2 days of sustained unnoticed trickle. | Foregrounded in `docs/bolt/theorem.md` §14.2 (patient bound derivation), §14.2.1 (degenerate sub-drift case), and the Claim section. Disclosed in `docs/evaluation.md` §8 item 4. The sub-drift scope limitation is stated as a scoping limitation, not a footnote. |
| L8 | **Simplified governance.** `RegistryV2` uses a static m-of-n governor set (immutable at construction, `m = 3`). No governor rotation, no approval expiry, no timelock. A lost governor key permanently consumes liveness margin. | `m` simultaneously compromised governors defeat the registry: identical exposure to any m-of-n multisig. Governor rotation and timelock are standard audited engineering (e.g. OpenZeppelin `TimelockController`) with no research content; adding them widens the PoC audit surface without strengthening any tested claim. | Explicitly scoped out in `docs/bolt/threat_model.md` §Governance scope. Production deployments should use an audited governance framework. Recorded as future work. |
| L9 | **Synthetic-controlled portions remain.** The bound-validation schedules (scenario 5, 45 schedules) and the H1 canonical low-and-slow drain are injected or synthetic. The composed Δ ≈ 60 s and δ_detect = 36.18 ms are single-transaction paths on a local or forked node. | The on-chain bound validation is against injected attack schedules, not a live adversary. Latency under network load, mempool contention, or multi-transaction concurrency is unmeasured. | Disclosed in `docs/bolt/theorem.md` §14.3 simulation scope, `docs/evaluation.md` §8 items 1, 9. The 45 hardhat-mode schedules validate the pathwise arrival-curve bound at the assumed parameters; they do not measure detection-delay distributions. |
| L10 | **Train→test drift breaks value baselines' FPR budgets.** All baselines are calibrated to the same 1% TRAIN benign-FPR budget, yet the value z-score, robust-MAD, and IsolationForest realise 4.6–18.2% φ on held-out TEST. Only the sequential CPD honours its budget out-of-sample on this split. | The comparison is not on equal footing in the test split for the budget-matched claim; only the CPD's stated φ = 0.0025 is a valid held-out budget figure. | Reported per row in `docs/logs/scenario_6_detection_eval.log`. Disclosed in `docs/evaluation.md` §8 item 6b and `docs/bolt/h1_scorecard.md` row 8 caveats. |
| L11 | **DID resolution is local; IPFS pinning not implemented.** The PoC stores a `keccak256` of the DID document on-chain but does not publish to IPFS or deploy the upstream `ethr-did-registry`. Phase 9c (IPFS pinning) is out of scope. | A third-party `did:ethr` resolver would not natively resolve PoC DIDs without configuration. The Protect bound holds on-chain, but the full DID-document lifecycle (publication, discovery, revocation propagation to off-chain resolvers) is incomplete. | Disclosed in `docs/evaluation.md` §4 item 5, §8. Tracked as a natural extension in `.superpowers/phases/phase-9-optional.md`. |
| L12 | **Blind approval of an opaque `opId` (governor diligence is assumed, not enforced).** `RegistryV2.approve(bytes32 opId)` binds an approval to a 32-byte hash, not to a human-readable action, and the contract cannot verify that the approver reconstructed the pre-image. Two properties compound it: approvals are irrevocable and never expire, so a mistaken approval cannot be withdrawn before execution; and `govNonce[validatorAddr]` is folded into every `opId` and advances on every executed op for that validator, so an unrelated op executing first silently voids the whole pending approval set and forces a fresh m-of-n round. | A governor who approves without recomputing `computeOpId(opType, addr, docHash, ttl)` off-chain may authorise an operation other than the one intended, and cannot take the approval back. The nonce quirk is a liveness cost rather than a safety one, but it bites hardest exactly where it matters least tolerably: an emergency revocation whose approvals are silently invalidated by a routine registration executing first. This is a conventional multisig governance-UX weakness, not a novel one; it is recorded because it was previously undisclosed. | Disclosed in the `approve` NatSpec (`contracts/RegistryV2.sol`) and in `docs/bolt/threat_model.md` (Governance scope). Not mitigated in the PoC: displaying decoded operation parameters in the `Proposed` event, approval expiry, and a timelock are the standard fixes and belong with the audited governance framework recorded under L8 as a production requirement. |
