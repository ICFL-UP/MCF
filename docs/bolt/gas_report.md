## Chosen parameter defaults (locked symbols, `docs/bolt/theorem.md`)

| Symbol | Meaning | Default | One-line justification |
|--------|---------|---------|------------------------|
| `B` | token-bucket burst capacity | **1000 ETH** | Caps any single block's admitted outflow; assumption 4 (no same-block escape). Far below Ronin's 173,600-ETH drain, so no single block can replay that loss. |
| `r` | token-bucket refill rate | **1 ETH/s** | ≈ 86,400 ETH/day of legitimate throughput headroom; sets the additive `r·Δ` slack term in both bound regimes. |
| `T` | per-window outflow threshold | **5000 ETH** | Breaker trips when window (`W = 3600 s`) outflow exceeds this; with `D = ARL₁ = 480 s` the expected patient bound `E[X_slow] ≤ B + r·D + r·Δ` stays orders of magnitude below 173,600 ETH. |

`W = 3600 s` (sliding window). These feed Phase 14 (bound numbers) and Phase 17 (reporting).

## Per-withdrawal overhead vs a bare transfer

A bare ETH transfer is **21,000 gas** (intrinsic). The instrumented BOLT exit
`withdraw(...)` at `k = 5` is **323,090 gas avg** (min 288,869 / max 354,167).
The ≈ **302,000 gas** of overhead above a bare send breaks down as:

| Component | Approx gas | What it buys |
|-----------|-----------:|--------------|
| 5× ECDSA `ecrecover` (k distinct signers) | ~5 × 3,000 = 15,000 | k-of-n consensus any bridge already pays |
| 5× per-withdrawal `verifyValidator` (G6) | dominant SLOAD cost | revocation/expiry awareness *every* withdrawal |
| RateLimiter `consume` | ~33,964 | provable per-block burst cap `B` + window |
| CircuitBreaker `checkAndRecord` | ~200k–260k (≤ 122 slot reads, fixed) | synchronous in-path window cap `T` + latch, CONSTANT in traffic volume (V2 1.7) |
| nonce write + ETH `.call` (CEI, last) | ~21,000+ | replay protection + reentrancy-safe transfer |

The dominant single line item is the breaker's fixed 61-slot bucket-window sum
(`checkAndRecord`), followed by the limiter `consume` and the k registry reads.
This is the price of moving the loss-bound enforcement *on-path* and synchronous
rather than relying on an off-chain monitor that arrives `δ_detect` late — and,
post-1.7, of making that enforcement gas-constant under sustained load (the old
array-pruning window averaged ~92k but grew without bound: 1,106,606 measured at
the 400th record, a gas-DoS vector the bucket accumulator removes).

## Reading the cost

The marginal cost of the BOLT machinery on the hot path is the limiter `consume`
(~34k) plus the breaker `checkAndRecord` (~200k–260k in-path, constant in traffic
volume) plus the k per-withdrawal `verifyValidator` reads, on top of the k ECDSA
recoveries that any k-of-n bridge already pays. This buys: a provable per-block burst
cap `B`, a synchronous in-path window cap `T` whose gas cannot be inflated by
sustained traffic (V2 1.7), a monitor kill-switch (`trip`), and per-withdrawal
revocation awareness (G6).

**Comparison to production bridge practice.** A production bridge withdrawal on Ethereum
typically costs $0.05–$0.50 depending on contract complexity and token type
(e.g. the post-2022 hardened Ronin bridge's ERC-20 withdraw path is in the 200–400k gas
range). BOLT's `withdraw` at 323,090 gas sits comfortably within that band. The dominant
marginal overhead is `checkAndRecord` (~282k gas), which is the in-path price of a
synchronous, gas-constant sliding-window cap — a design choice that eliminates the
gas-DoS vector of the earlier array implementation without materially changing the
per-transaction cost at the stated illustrative assumptions.
